  1. 01

    Question 01 of 052020Define improvement

    Better soil — compared with what?

    The Soil Health Gap frames soil condition as a distance from an undisturbed reference within an agroecosystem. It gives improvement a benchmark, rather than treating an isolated test result as a verdict.

    Leaves openThe reference and indicator matter; a gap is not a universal soil-health score.

    Published paper

    Soil Health Gap: A concept to establish a benchmark for soil health management

    Maharjan, Das & Acharya · Global Ecology and Conservation · 2020

  2. 02

    Question 02 of 052022Make comparison fair

    Which soils belong in the same comparison?

    The Cropland Reference Ecological Unit (CREU) groups land with comparable soil-forming and climatic conditions. It helps separate differences caused by management from differences a field inherits from its environment.

    Leaves openCREU supplies ecological context; it does not make every soil or management history identical.

    Published paper

    Cropland Reference Ecological Unit: A land classification unit for comparative soil studies

    Das & Maharjan · Ecological Indicators · 2022

  3. 03

    Question 03 of 052024Choose the benchmark

    What makes a reference defensible?

    Reference-site selection uses ecological site descriptions and state-and-transition models to identify the appropriate reference plant community. The benchmark becomes an ecological condition to establish, rather than the nearest patch that looks undisturbed.

    Leaves openReference selection still requires field evidence and knowledge of disturbance history.

    Published paper

    Reference site selection based on state-and-transition models for soil health gap evaluation within cropland reference ecological units

    Das, Hird, Maharjan, Stephenson & Kariyawasam · Soil Security · 2024

  4. 04

    Question 04 of 052026Measure & interpret

    Can measurement be consistent and interpretation local?

    Tier 1 identifies a practical core: organic matter, pH, nitrate-N, and bulk density. Tier 2 interprets those measurements against ecologically matched reference distributions. The framework separates what to measure from what a result means in a particular soil and climate.

    Leaves openThe reference dataset comes from Nebraska; broader transfer needs regional testing and calibration.

    Published paper

    A two-tier, reference-informed framework for soil health assessment: Minimum indicator sets and ecologically dynamic thresholds

    Das, Gamage, Stephenson, Acharya, Daigh & Maharjan · Agrosystems, Geosciences & Environment · 2026

  5. 05

    Question 05 of 052026Find the next stepPreprint

    How do we turn potential into attainable progress?

    Hierarchical Land Classification connects ecological reference, recovery, soil-building, production, and degraded states. A synthesis of 1,059 soil organic carbon observations from 105 studies examines intermediate targets between today's condition and ecological potential.

    Leaves openPreprint. Recovery and soil-building states were not statistically distinguishable; targets are neither universal nor equally spaced.

    Preprint · not peer reviewed

    Hierarchical Land Classification for Soil Health Assessment: Linking Reference Potential, Management State, and Incremental Soil Health Targets

02The journey

Water, then soil, then systems.

Three places that changed what I ask and how I work.

  1. Chapter 012012–2017

    Water

    Learning to see the invisible

    Assam · Assam Agricultural University

    What is happening in the water we cannot see?

    Doctoral work in agricultural biotechnology took me into arsenic biogeochemistry, groundwater, and microbial communities in Assam. It built a habit that still shapes my research: look beneath the visible symptom and ask which living processes are driving it.

    What I carried forward

    Follow the microbes. Pay attention to context. Start with a real problem in the field.

    GroundwaterMicrobial ecologyArsenic

    Explore the groundwater study ↗

  2. Chapter 022018–2024

    Soil

    Learning to ask “compared with what?”

    Nebraska · University of Nebraska–Lincoln

    What does healthy soil mean in this particular field?

    Postdoctoral and research faculty work moved those questions into agricultural soils: benchmarking, carbon and nitrogen, and the effects of management. Different soils kept resisting the same scorecard. That tension runs through the Soil Health Gap, ecological units, and reference-site work.

    What I carried forward

    A measurement needs a fair comparison. A field has both a history and a potential.

    Soil healthCarbon & nitrogenReference sites

    Systems

    Making the knowledge useful

    Long-term systems · Long-term farming systems research

    How does evidence become something a farmer can use?

    The journey now connects long-term farming systems research with practical indicators, attainable targets, and tools that make evidence easier to use. The same curiosity extends into building: how can careful science travel beyond a paper and become part of an everyday decision?

03Other threads in the work

Mechanisms, microbes & the bigger picture.

6 more papers around the thread: evidence, mechanisms, and where soil health claims hold up.

  1. 2026Position paper

    Soil Health in Practice: Principles, Proof, and the Path Forward ↗

    A systems-oriented look at how soil health moved from niche concept to organizing framework, and what kinds of evidence are still needed to connect management practices to measurable outcomes.

  2. 2025Sustainability

    Soil Carbon Sequestration: A Mechanistic Perspective on Limitations and Future Possibilities ↗

    Examines where soil carbon narratives are scientifically strong, where they become overstated, and what mechanistic understanding is required for credible long-term sequestration claims.

  3. 2024Agrosystems, Geosciences & Environment

    Soil Health Cycle ↗

    A framework for thinking about soil health as an iterative management cycle linking practices, indicators, functions, and human decision-making rather than as a one-time score.

  4. 2024PLOS One

    Nitrogen Dynamics as a Function of Soil Types, Compaction, and Moisture ↗

    Investigates how compaction and moisture regimes shape nitrogen transformations, leaching, volatilization, and emissions across contrasting soil textures.

  5. 2018BMC Microbiology

    Characterization of Siderophore Producing Arsenic-Resistant Staphylococcus sp. Strain TA6 ↗

    A study of arsenic-resistant bacteria from contaminated groundwater in Assam and their possible role in the local arsenic geocycle.

  6. 2017Genomics Data

    A Metagenomic Approach to Decipher the Indigenous Microbial Communities of Arsenic Contaminated Groundwater of Assam ↗

    An early metagenomic investigation into the microbial communities inhabiting arsenic-affected groundwater systems, reflecting the foundation of my research journey.
